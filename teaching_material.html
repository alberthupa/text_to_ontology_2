03 — From text to RDF · 1/11

Start with one input sentence

We will keep the same tiny example all the way from raw text to RDF, RDFS, a knowledge graph, SHACL, and SPARQL.
“There was a conference in Paris.”

Thing 1

A conference
An event mentioned in the sentence.

Relationship

Was held in
How the two things are connected.

Thing 2

Paris
A place mentioned in the sentence.

First mental model: conference → held in → Paris
04 — From text to RDF · 2/11

Give the things identifiers

Computers need identifiers that do not depend on spelling or language. In RDF, these global identifiers are IRIs.
@prefix ex: <http://example.org/> .

Short identifiers

ex:conference-1
ex:heldIn
ex:paris

Their complete IRIs

http://example.org/conference-1
http://example.org/heldIn
http://example.org/paris
A prefix only makes an IRI easier to read. It does not change the identifier.
05 — From text to RDF · 3/11

Create the first RDF triple

RDF represents a statement with three parts: subject, predicate, and object. RDF calls this a triple.
ex:conference-1 ex:heldIn ex:paris .
Subjectex:conference-1
→
Predicateex:heldIn
→
Objectex:paris
Read it as: “Conference 1 was held in Paris.” The period ends the triple.
06 — From text to RDF · 4/11

Declare the classes with RDFS

RDFS adds a small schema vocabulary on top of RDF: classes, labels, hierarchies, domain, and range.
@prefix rdfs: <http://www.w3.org/2000/01/rdf-schema#> .

ex:Event a rdfs:Class ;
    rdfs:label "Event" .

ex:Conference a rdfs:Class ;
    rdfs:label "Conference" .

ex:Place a rdfs:Class ;
    rdfs:label "Place" .

The semicolon

Continue describing the same subject with another predicate and object.

What we created

Three reusable categories: Event, Conference, and Place.

07 — From text to RDF · 5/11

Create a class hierarchy

A hierarchy lets a reasoner derive a more general type from a more specific one.

What we state

ex:Conference
    rdfs:subClassOf ex:Event .

ex:conference-1
    a ex:Conference .

What RDFS can infer

ex:conference-1
    a ex:Event .
Reasoning: every Conference is an Event; Conference 1 is a Conference; therefore Conference 1 is an Event.
08 — From text to RDF · 6/11

Describe the relationship

RDFS domain and range tell a reasoner what types of resources a property connects. 
@prefix rdf: <http://www.w3.org/1999/02/22-rdf-syntax-ns#> .

ex:heldIn
    a rdf:Property ;
    rdfs:label "held in" ;
    rdfs:domain ex:Event ;
    rdfs:range ex:Place .

ex:conference-1 ex:heldIn ex:paris .

Domain inference

ex:conference-1 is an Event.

Range inference

ex:paris is a Place.

Important: domain and range support inference. They are not validation rules.
09 — From text to RDF · 7/11

Put the ontology and facts together

The ontology defines reusable meaning. The knowledge graph uses that vocabulary to describe particular things.

Ontology · vocabulary

ex:Event a rdfs:Class .

ex:Conference
    a rdfs:Class ;
    rdfs:subClassOf ex:Event .

ex:Place a rdfs:Class .
ex:heldIn a rdf:Property .

Knowledge graph · facts

ex:conference-1
    a ex:Conference ;
    rdfs:label "Conference 1" ;
    ex:heldIn ex:paris .

ex:paris
    a ex:Place ;
    rdfs:label "Paris" .
Ontology: what kinds of things and relationships exist. Knowledge graph: what we know about particular things.
10 — From text to RDF · 8/11

Add a SHACL validation rule

Our data requirement: every Conference must have at least one heldIn relationship.
@prefix sh:
  <http://www.w3.org/ns/shacl#> .

ex:ConferenceShape
    a sh:NodeShape ;
    sh:targetClass ex:Conference ;
    sh:property [
        sh:path ex:heldIn ;
        sh:minCount 1
    ] .

Read the shape

  • NodeShape — validate resources.
  • targetClass — check every Conference.
  • path — inspect the heldIn property.
  • minCount 1 — require at least one value.
The ontology describes meaning. The SHACL shape states an acceptance criterion.
11 — From text to RDF · 9/11

Validate conforming data

Conference 1 has the required relationship, so it satisfies the shape.

Data

ex:conference-1
    a ex:Conference ;
    ex:heldIn ex:paris .

SHACL result

Conforms: true

The conference has at least one heldIn value.

Conference 1 → held in → Paris passes the rule.
12 — From text to RDF · 10/11

Validate non-conforming data

Conference 2 has no heldIn relationship, so SHACL reports the missing required value.

Data

ex:conference-2
    a ex:Conference ;
    rdfs:label "Conference 2" .

SHACL result

Conforms: false

Required ex:heldIn value is missing.

RDFS supports inference: “What else follows?”   SHACL validates: “Does this data satisfy our requirements?”
13 — From text to RDF · 11/11

Query the knowledge graph

SPARQL uses graph patterns. The variable ?place asks RDF to find the missing part of our triple.
PREFIX ex: <http://example.org/>

SELECT ?place
WHERE {
    ex:conference-1
        ex:heldIn ?place .
}

Question

Where was Conference 1 held?

Result

ex:paris
RDF states the fact · RDFS defines basic meaning · SHACL validates it · SPARQL retrieves it.

17 — Vocabulary landscape · active standards

The standards layer is active again

RDF 1.2 Concepts reached Candidate Recommendation in April 2026; Turtle, SHACL 1.2, and SPARQL 1.2 remain active W3C drafts. They are relevant and still evolving—not yet finished Recommendations.
Working Draft

SHACL 1.2

Core, rules, node expressions, SPARQL extensions, profiling, compact syntax, and UI work. The practical bridge from semantics to executable acceptance rules.

Working Draft

SPARQL 1.2

The query language evolves alongside RDF 1.2 and its new triple-term model.

RDF 1.2 Turtle · Working Draft

Metadata about a claim

VERSION "1.2"

:alice :worksFor :acme .

<< :alice :worksFor :acme
   ~ :claim123 >>
    :confidence 0.92 ;
    :source :SAP .

Current RDF 1.2 terminology distinguishes triple terms and reified triples. This is valuable for claims, evidence, confidence, and provenance.

18 — RDF-star → RDF 1.2

Give a statement its own metadata

A normal RDF triple states a fact. RDF-star introduced a compact way to add a source, time, confidence, or other metadata to that fact. RDF 1.2 brings this idea into the core RDF model with precise triple-term and reifier semantics.
Earlier RDF-star notation

Describe the relationship itself

:conference-1 ex:heldIn :paris .

<< :conference-1 ex:heldIn :paris >>
    ex:confidence 0.92 ;
    ex:source :report-7 .

The metadata is about “Conference 1 was held in Paris”—not about the conference or Paris on its own. This avoids the four-part classic reification pattern.

RDF 1.2 Turtle · Working Draft

Assert and annotate the fact

VERSION "1.2"

:conference-1 ex:heldIn :paris
  ~ :claim-7 {|
    ex:confidence 0.92 ;
    ex:source :report-7
  |} .

This shortcut asserts the fact, names its reifier :claim-7, and attaches the metadata. Underneath, the reifier points to a triple term:

:claim-7 rdf:reifies
  <<( :conference-1 ex:heldIn :paris )>> .
The relationship: RDF-star is the proposal lineage; RDF 1.2 is its standards-track destination. A triple term denotes the proposition; a reifier is the claim or occurrence carrying metadata. In RDF 1.2, a reified triple alone does not assert the fact; the annotation form above does both.
